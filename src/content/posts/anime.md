I was introduced to anime informally through classics like *Doraemon* and *Shin-chan*. Watching them familiarized me with Japanese culture at a very young age. Seeing the characters eat with chopsticks or casually buy snacks from a vending machine was fascinating to me; where I live, vending machines are a luxury found only in high-end airports. It even baffled my tiny brain that you could sleep on a floor or inside a cupboard. Even at that age, I preferred the beautiful storytelling of anime over whatever standard cartoons we were being served.

As I got older, my interest only grew. My older brother eventually introduced me to "proper" anime with the film *Suzume*, and boy, was it good. I went on to watch several Ghibli films with my older brother, and eventually, my first anime series—the kind most people think of when they hear the word. That was *Demon Slayer* (though I had watched *Solo Leveling* before that, but apparently, that is Korean)
I am currently on my 4th anime, *Jujutsu Kaisen*, and it’s been a great watch. It’s not philosophical or mythical like the Ghibli movies, but the storyline is solid and it’s left me wanting more. Before this, I watched *Frieren*, which was a total shocker; it didn’t feel 'Japanese' at all, but rather had more of a European vibe. It really showed that anime isn't just about looking cool; it can be peaceful. It was refreshing to see a different take on the classic "happily ever after" trope.
